Pune Startup Launches AI Tool to Bring Healthcare Support to Rural Clinics

A Pune-based startup called MediSense Labs announced on October 5, 2026 that it has launched an artificial intelligence tool designed to help doctors in rural clinics diagnose common diseases faster. The platform, named GramAI, uses machine learning to analyse patient symptoms, blood test values and chest X-ray images, and then suggests possible conditions for the doctor to review. Company founder Dr. Ananya Deshmukh said the tool was built to reduce the long waiting times that patients face in villages where specialist doctors are rarely available.

According to the company, GramAI was tested for eight months in 40 primary health centres across Maharashtra and Karnataka. During the pilot, the system analysed more than 25,000 patient records. Researchers from the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay reviewed the results and found that the tool matched the opinion of senior doctors in nearly nine out of ten cases. The pilot was supported by a grant of Rs 12 crore from the Maharashtra State Innovation Society.

Experts say that artificial intelligence can play a major role in healthcare, but they also warn about its limits. Professor Rajiv Menon, a data scientist at the Indian Institute of Science in Bengaluru, said that AI systems should always support doctors and never replace them. He explained that the quality of a machine learning model depends on the quality of its training data, and that poor data can lead to biased or incorrect results. For this reason, GramAI shows a confidence score with every suggestion and asks the doctor to confirm the final decision.

The startup plans to expand the tool to 500 clinics by March 2027. It will also release a Hindi and Marathi voice interface so that health workers can enter symptoms by speaking instead of typing. Deshmukh said the company will present its results at the Digital Health Summit in New Delhi on November 20, 2026. She added that patient data is encrypted and stored on servers inside India to protect privacy.

Health officials welcomed the announcement. A spokesperson for the National Health Authority said the government is open to technology that improves access to care, provided that it follows strict safety and privacy rules. Many rural clinics still struggle with a shortage of trained staff, and the spokesperson noted that digital tools may help bridge this gap in the coming years. Doctors who used the pilot version said the tool saved them time and helped them focus on patients who needed urgent attention.
